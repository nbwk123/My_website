<section class="case-dark-intro more-design-dark" markdown="1">

<p class="case-section-label">Core Idea</p>

## The page should feel designed, but the design should point back to the work.

Good personal sites are not only portfolios. They are learning records, project shelves, research notebooks, and small publishing systems. The visual layer matters because it helps people trust the structure underneath.

<div class="case-flow-list" markdown="1">
<section markdown="1">
<span>01</span>
### Notice
Capture useful moments from courses, projects, readings, and travel.
</section>

<section markdown="1">
<span>02</span>
### Sort
Place each piece of content in a clear category before styling it.
</section>

<section markdown="1">
<span>03</span>
### Shape
Choose layout, rhythm, and emphasis based on what the reader needs.
</section>

<section markdown="1">
<span>04</span>
### Test
Check whether the page is readable, usable, and consistent across languages.
</section>

<section markdown="1">
<span>05</span>
### Iterate
Keep refining details without losing the original structure.
</section>
</div>

</section>

<section class="case-section" markdown="1">

<div markdown="1">
<p class="case-section-label">Structure</p>

## From isolated pages to a personal system.

Each page should have a clear role. The homepage creates direction, course pages hold learning records, project pages show practice, blog pages keep experiences, and the About page explains the person behind the work.
</div>

<div class="case-opportunity-grid" markdown="1">
<article markdown="1">
### Entry
Help readers understand where they are and where to go next.
</article>

<article markdown="1">
### Continuity
Keep navigation, spacing, and page rhythm consistent across sections.
</article>

<article markdown="1">
### Memory
Let older notes remain findable as the site grows.
</article>
</div>

</section>

<section class="case-section case-section-flip" markdown="1">

<div markdown="1">
<p class="case-section-label">Practice</p>

## Design decisions become easier when the page has a job.

Instead of asking whether an element looks interesting, I want to ask whether it helps the page do its job: introduce a topic, compare ideas, show evidence, or invite the next action.
</div>

<div class="case-detail-grid" markdown="1">
<article markdown="1">
### Navigation
The reader should always know the current section and have a simple way to move.
</article>

<article markdown="1">
### Hierarchy
Headings, tags, images, and notes should form a calm reading order.
</article>

<article markdown="1">
### Interaction
Motion and clickable modules should reveal information rather than distract.
</article>

<article markdown="1">
### Maintenance
The structure should still work after new courses, projects, and notes are added.
</article>
</div>

</section>

<section class="case-reflection more-design-reflection" markdown="1">

<p class="case-section-label">Next Direction</p>

## Keep building a site that can hold both work and thought.

The next step is to make every section feel connected: project details should explain process, blog pages should preserve lived experience, and the About area should make the whole site feel personal without becoming decorative.

</section>
